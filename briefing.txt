Markets Briefing - Thursday 11 June 2026

Risk tone is cautious to risk-off this morning. US equities fell sharply on Wednesday after May CPI hit a three-year high of 4.2% and a second consecutive night of US military strikes on Iran pushed Brent crude above $95. The single most important event today is the ECB rate decision at approximately 14:15 CET, where a first rate hike in roughly a year is widely expected, with May US PPI due simultaneously at 14:30 CET.

Overnight headlines
US Central Command completed a second night of strikes on Iranian targets including air defense sites, communication systems, and surveillance assets. Iran has declared the Strait of Hormuz closed to commercial traffic; CENTCOM disputes a full closure, saying commercial ships continue to transit. Qatar is in Tehran for diplomatic talks. May CPI printed at 4.2% year-on-year, the highest in three years, with core monthly at 0.2%, slightly softer than feared but not enough to shift the rate hiking narrative.

PRIVATE EQUITY OVERLAY

Financing
Three-month Euribor is around 2.80-2.82% and will edge higher following today's expected ECB hike; money markets see rates approaching 2.5-2.75% on the deposit facility by year-end. Term SOFR levels were not confirmed in real-time sources this morning. Leveraged loan spreads for BB and B credits are near cycle lows but are expected to widen 25-50bp over 2026, especially for weaker credits and energy-exposed sectors. HY OAS at 285bp and IG at 80bp signal that all-in financing costs are rising from recent tights. Private credit lenders face renewed competition as syndicated banks push back into sponsor-led deals, compressing direct lending margins.

Comps and exits
Adobe's post-close earnings will be a read-across for software sector multiples after the stock rallied roughly 8% in early June on rotation into beaten-down software. The broader IPO exit window is cautiously open but energy inflation and elevated rates are limiting the timing of sponsor exits. Public EV/EBITDA multiples in tech and industrials are under mild pressure following Wednesday's CPI shock.

Deal and sector flow
No landmark sponsor transactions have been announced this morning. The 2026 leveraged finance and LBO calendar remains active in aggregate, continuing a recovery from the 2024-2025 slowdown; however, near-term risk appetite in live sponsor processes is sensitive to today's ECB tone and PPI outcome.

BOTTOM LINE
The ECB hike, sticky US inflation, and an Iran conflict showing no near-term resolution collectively tighten financial conditions and compress deal economics at the margin; the Lagarde press conference guidance and the PPI print are the two data points to watch before the European close today.
