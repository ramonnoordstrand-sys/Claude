PRIVATE EQUITY OVERLAY

Financing: Euribor 3M is estimated near 2.25% following the June 11 ECB hike (from 2.08% in March). Term SOFR remains relevant for dollar paper. Private credit all-in yields in Europe run 8.5-11% for senior secured, 10-13.5% for unitranche. Leveraged loan and HY markets are open but conditions have tightened since the conflict began in late February -- pricing is up across the board, diligence timelines are longer, and lenders are scrutinising energy-cost and supply-chain exposure more carefully. Direct lenders are still clearing deals for quality sponsors in defensible sectors.

Comps and exits: EV/EBITDA multiples have compressed in energy-exposed and consumer-facing names, while healthcare and business services multiples are holding up. The IPO window is effectively closed; strategic M&A is the primary exit route. Bid-ask gaps on large-cap buyouts remain wide.

Deal flow: H1 2026 deal volume is running roughly 34% below H1 2025, but aggregate deal value is up about 10%, reflecting concentration in larger, higher-conviction transactions. Healthcare take-privates and roll-ups, business services, and industrials mid-market buy-and-builds are the most active segments. Tech and consumer are quiet given AI-driven valuation uncertainty and margin pressure.

Bottom line: Hormuz progress is the most meaningful positive shift in the macro backdrop this month -- if today's PMI readings confirm that European activity is holding, it supports deal underwriting assumptions; a weak Germany print, by contrast, would extend the pause in exit-market activity and keep financing conditions tight.
