Overnight headlines
US CENTCOM struck Iranian military targets; Iran retaliated against Gulf state facilities. Markets have shifted from pricing a near-term ceasefire to pricing a prolonged conflict with intermittent exchanges. Fitch downgraded its global sovereign sector outlook to "deteriorating," citing the US-Iran war, elevated inflation, and higher bond yields as structural risks. Oil is holding below $100 despite escalation, providing some limit to the macro damage. AI infrastructure demand continues to support tech equity valuations and act as a counterweight to risk-off flows.

PE OVERLAY

Financing
3M Euribor implied around 2.5-2.7% following the ECB's June hike. Term SOFR broadly around 3.0-3.25% in line with the Fed cutting path. Leveraged loan and high-yield new issuance is clearing with cautious discipline; private credit remains active and filling gaps left by banks pulling back. Tight IG spreads support all-in refinancing costs for investment-grade rated portfolio companies.

Comps and exits
EV/EBITDA multiples remain elevated in technology and infrastructure but have compressed in consumer and industrial sectors. The IPO window is partially open but geopolitical volatility is limiting sponsor confidence in timing. Strategic M&A continues to be the preferred exit route for most sponsors.

Deal and sector flow
2025 sponsor-led deal volume reached $1.2 trillion, the second-highest on record. 2026 activity continues with discipline: a Deloitte survey of 500 PE and corporate leaders flags cautious optimism, with cross-border acquisitions as the key growth vector. Sector focus concentrated in AI infrastructure, energy transition, and healthcare services.

BOTTOM LINE
The Iran escalation is the single biggest regime variable today: it lifts energy costs and compresses growth expectations, but oil holding below $100 and historically tight credit spreads mean the immediate damage to deal-making and portfolio company fundamentals is contained unless the conflict broadens materially.
